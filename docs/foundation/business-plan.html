1. Executive Summary

NaijaJobber is a two-sided employment marketplace built for the African remote-work economy. The platform serves job seekers with profile depth, AI-assisted CV tools, applications, interviews, learning, and wallet/referrals; and serves employers with company verification, job publishing, applicant pipeline, team management, billing, and analytics.

Target market
Africa → Global
Revenue model
SaaS + fees
Stage
MVP live

Our mission is to reduce friction between verified African talent and trustworthy global employers while creating auditable impact metrics suitable for grant reporting (placements, remote jobs, verified employers, countries reached).

2. Problem Statement

  • Fragmented hiring: Job seekers juggle informal channels; employers lack structured pipelines and verification.
  • Trust gap: Cross-border hiring requires employer verification, document review, and moderation — rarely available in generalist boards.
  • Preparation gap: Candidates lack accessible CV, interview, and learning tools aligned to remote roles.
  • Impact opacity: Funders and partners need KPIs (hires, remote roles, demographics) that legacy tools do not surface.

NaijaJobber delivers end-to-end workflows in a single platform:

4. Market Opportunity

Africa’s youth workforce is expanding while remote-first hiring accelerates globally. Nigeria and broader Anglophone Africa represent a large pool of tech, creative, and professional talent seeking USD/EUR-denominated remote roles. Employers seek cost-efficient, English-proficient talent with verifiable profiles and structured application data.

Initial beachhead

  • Nigeria-based job seekers and diaspora-friendly remote roles
  • SME and growth-stage employers hiring 1–50 remote roles/year
  • Grant-aligned workforce development programs needing placement metrics

6. Go-to-Market Strategy

  1. Supply (seekers): University partnerships, referral program, learning content, social proof from placements.
  2. Demand (employers): Outbound to remote-friendly companies; verification-led trust; case studies on time-to-hire.
  3. Ops excellence: Moderation queue, support SLAs, finance ops for subscriptions/refunds.
  4. Grant narrative: Publish impact dashboard (seekers, remote jobs, hires, countries, women/youth provisional metrics).

7. Competitive Advantage

  • Purpose-built for African remote employment (not a generic global board)
  • Integrated verification, moderation, and admin/ops tooling
  • AI-assisted candidate tools with mock-safe demo mode for partners
  • Grant-ready impact and exportable reports (CSV/JSON)
  • Phased, production-grade architecture (NestJS modules, RBAC, audit logs)

9. Team & Operations

Founding team covers product engineering, with planned hires in community/growth, employer success, and support operations. Platform roles (Moderator, Support Agent, Finance Manager, Marketing Manager) are modeled in-product for scalable ops.

10. Financial Summary

Year 1 focuses on product-market fit with controlled burn. Primary costs: cloud infrastructure, part-time ops, marketing, and compliance. Revenue ramps from employer subscriptions in months 6–12. See companion Financial Model and Grant Budget Narrative for detail.

Funding use (illustrative): 40% product & engineering · 25% go-to-market · 20% operations & support · 10% compliance & legal · 5% contingency
